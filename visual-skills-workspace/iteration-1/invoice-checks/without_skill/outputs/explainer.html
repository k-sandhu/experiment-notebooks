Accounts payable automation, tested on 72 invoices

When does a cheap AI first pass pay for itself?

An AI model that decides whether to pay an invoice, and how much, can be cheap and often wrong, or expensive and reliable. A middle route is to let the cheap model answer, check that answer, and send only the doubtful invoices to the expensive model. This page walks through what happened when that was tried on 72 test invoices, then lets you set the price of the expensive model and see which route costs least.

Three ways to get every invoice right

Each invoice needs one of two answers: hold it (a duplicate, a blocked supplier, or an amount over the limit without a valid signed exception), or pay an exact amount. An answer counts as right only if the decision and the amount both match to the cent.

Expensive on every invoice

Send all invoices to the expensive model

GPT-6.1 Sol reads each invoice. You pay the expensive price 72 times out of 72.

Cheap + ask-twice check

Ask the cheap model twice

Qwen3.8 Flash answers the same invoice twice in separate calls. If both answers agree on the decision and the amount to the cent, pay it. If not, send it to the expensive model.

Cheap + reviewer check

Have a strong model review it

Claude Opus 5.5 reads the invoice and the cheap model's answer, and accepts or rejects it. Rejected invoices go to the expensive model.

The break-even price

Both checks save the same thing: 14 expensive calls out of 72, or 0.194 calls per invoice. They differ in what the check itself costs. A check pays for itself once the expensive calls it avoids are worth more than it costs:

Ask-twice check

Two cheap-model calls per invoice
cost $0.000218 per invoice ÷ 0.194 calls saved
= $0.0011 per expensive call

If the expensive model costs more than about a tenth of a cent per call, asking twice is cheaper than sending everything to it.

Reviewer check

One cheap-model call plus one Claude Opus 5.5 review per invoice, at list price
cost $0.0259 per invoice ÷ 0.194 calls saved
= $0.133 per expensive call

The review costs about 120 times as much as asking twice, so the expensive model must cost more than 13 cents per call before it pays.

Before you rely on this

  • The checks never met a hard case. The cheap model was right only on the 14 invoices that should be held, and wrong by tens to thousands of dollars on every invoice it should have paid. Both checks caught every mistake and wasted no escalations. With 72 out of 72 correct, the test cannot tell the two checks apart on accuracy, and a check that lets a wrong payment through would change the break-even.
  • The ask-twice check works because it demands agreement to the cent. Accepting answers that agree within $1 would have paid 8 wrong amounts.
  • Only 14 of 72 invoices (19.4%) were saved from the expensive model. Your saving depends on how often the cheap model is right on your invoices. Here it was right on none of the payable ones.
  • Prices are partly assumed. The expensive model's price per call is unknown, the reviewer is costed at list price, and the person who reviews invoices in the last route is an assumed $1 per invoice who is always right. Losses on wrong payments are assumed at 1% of overpayments and 0.1% of underpayments.
  • The invoices were generated by a program, so the exact answers are known; a simple rules program also gets all 72 right. The cheap model ran without step-by-step reasoning or a calculator.
