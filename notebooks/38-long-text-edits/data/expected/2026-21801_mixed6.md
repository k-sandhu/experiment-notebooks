# NRC Reviews of Reactor Designs Previously Authorized by U.S. Department of Energy or Department of War

## AGENCY:

Nuclear Regulatory Commission.

## ACTION:

Final rule.

## SUMMARY:

The U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) is amending its regulations to facilitate direct leveraging of prior U.S. Department of Energy or Department of War authorizations of demonstration reactors into the NRC's licensing reviews of commercial reactor facility applications that reference those designs. This rulemaking improves NRC licensing review efficiency, where applicable, by explicitly establishing by regulation an additional means for reactor applicants to demonstrate the safety functions of their reactor designs, thereby contributing to the safe and secure use and deployment of civilian nuclear energy technologies.

## DATES:

This final rule is effective on November 25, 2026.

## I. Executive Order 14300: Ordering the Reform of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission

On May 23, 2025, President Donald J. Trump signed Executive Order (E.O.) 14300, “Ordering the Reform of the Nuclear Regulatory Commission.” Section 5, “Reforming and Modernizing the NRC's Regulations,” directed the NRC to undertake a review and wholesale revision of its regulations and guidance documents as guided by the policies set forth in section 2 of the E.O. This rulemaking addresses section 5(d), which contemplates an expedited pathway to approve reactor designs that the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) or the Department of War (DOW) have tested and that have demonstrated the ability to function safely. E.O. 14300 further states that NRC review of such designs should focus solely on risks that may arise from new applications permitted by NRC licensure, rather than revisiting risks that have already been addressed in the DOE or DOW processes.

## II. Discussion

Consistent with its authority and historical practice under section 161 of the Atomic Energy Act of 1954 (AEA) to make, promulgate, issue, rescind, and amend rules and regulations as well as that under section 182 of the AEA to determine the information that enables the Commission to make the findings required to grant an application for a utilization facility, the NRC is amending title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations (10 CFR) section 50.43, “Additional standards and provisions affecting class 103 licenses and certifications for commercial power,” and section 53.440, “Design requirements.” These changes explicitly establish a pathway for streamlined reviews for licensing commercial reactor designs that have previously received DOE or DOW authorization and that have been tested and have demonstrated the ability to function safely. The amendment to § 50.43 explicitly establishes such a pathway for the licensing frameworks under 10 CFR part 50, “Domestic Licensing of Production and Utilization Facilities,” and part 52, “Licenses, Certifications, and Approvals for Nuclear Power Plants,” and the amendment to § 53.440 does so for the 10 CFR part 53, “Risk-Informed, Technology-Inclusive Regulatory Framework for Commercial Nuclear Plants,” licensing framework. A similar provision is included in a separate proposed rule, “Licensing Requirements for Microreactors and Other Reactors With Comparable Risk Profiles,” which, if finalized, would apply to the associated proposed 10 CFR part 57, “Licensing Requirements for Microreactors and Other Reactors with Comparable Risk Profiles,” licensing framework (91 FR 23628; May 1, 2026). The primary intent of this rulemaking is to improve NRC licensing review efficiency, and in turn increase capacity, for conducting licensing reviews. At the same time, this rulemaking maintains flexibility for the NRC to leverage a broad range of technologies, testing methods, and information from previously authorized and evaluated designs, as appropriate and supported by an applicant in its commercial license application. In turn, this contributes to the safe and secure use and deployment of civilian nuclear energy technologies.

Section 50.43(e) provides two options for license applicants to demonstrate how newer reactor designs, including those that use simplified, inherent, passive, or other innovative means, accomplish their safety functions. The final rule adds the option, to be codified in § 50.43(f), for an applicant to reference information from a prior DOE or DOW authorization as a means for an NRC license applicant to show that the design can accomplish its safety functions, as required by § 50.43(e).

NRC has examined this final rule and has determined that it is consistent with the policies and directives outlined in E.O. 14154.

The amendment to § 50.43 explicitly reflects the option for NRC Class 103 license applicants to directly reference a prior DOE or DOW authorization when demonstrating compliance with NRC regulatory requirements. Applicants using this option are required to identify how aspects of the prior authorization satisfy NRC regulations, including § 50.43(e). Applicants are also required to address how any changes to the design, its functionality, associated hazards, siting information, or underlying safety assumptions from those considered in prior authorization reviews meet applicable NRC requirements.

Under the part 53 licensing framework, § 53.440 provides a series of design requirements. The requirements in § 53.440(a)(1) reflect an essential element of ensuring a proposed design can comply with the performance criteria in part 53, namely that the abilities of design features to fulfill their safety functions are demonstrated by a combination of analyses, test programs, prototype testing, and operating experience. This requirement closely aligns with the language in § 50.43(e) and reflects the same foundational requirement.

The amendment to § 53.440(a)(1) explicitly accommodates the option for NRC Class 103 license applicants to directly reference a prior DOE or DOW authorization for the purpose of demonstrating that the design features required by § 53.400, “Design features for licensing-basis events” meet the defined functional design criteria required by §§ 53.410, “Functional design criteria for design-basis accidents,” and 53.420, “Functional design criteria for licensing-basis events other than design-basis accidents.” Such a demonstration is required to consider interdependent effects throughout the commercial nuclear plant and the range of conditions under which the design features required by § 53.400 must function throughout the plant's lifetime. Applicants using this option are required to identify how aspects of the prior authorization satisfy applicable NRC regulations.

The new §§ 50.43(f) and 53.440(a)(1) pathways will be supported by the draft Interim Staff Guidance (ISG), DANU-ISG-2026-XX, “NRC Application Pathway for Reactor Designs Previously Authorized by U.S. Department of Energy or Department of War.” The guidance is not part of this rulemaking but was published for public comment on April 17, 2026 (91 FR 20717). The scope of such guidance is intended to facilitate staff review of future commercial nuclear plant license applications, including advanced reactor designs that may not employ light-water technology.

The NRC also recognizes significant public interest in this rule and has provided a separate public comment response document as discussed in Section IV of this document, “Public Comment Analysis.” While that document provides comprehensive responses to comments, several points are highlighted below.

Some comments expressed concern that this rulemaking would impact the NRC's performance of independent evaluations of license applications mandated under the AEA. Specifically, section 103 of the AEA and title II of the Energy Reorganization Act of 1974 (88 Stat. 1242) authorize the NRC to, among other things, issue licenses for commercial nuclear power plants if in the opinion of the Commission, issuance of the license would not be inimical to the common defense and security or to the health and safety of the public. First and foremost, this rulemaking does not affect the NRC's commitment to conducting independent reviews, nor does this rulemaking alter any underlying regulatory requirements an applicant must meet. Nothing in this rule delegates to DOE, DOW, or any other agency the NRC's functions to evaluate a request for NRC approval, and nothing in this rule allows the NRC to forego an independent review. Regardless of whether an applicant references a prior DOE or DOW authorization, it remains the applicant's responsibility to demonstrate compliance with all applicable requirements, under the AEA and NRC regulations, and the NRC will continue to conduct the independent assessments necessary to confirm whether such requirements have indeed been satisfied. Where an NRC applicant references information from a prior design authorized and tested by DOE or DOW, the applicant is responsible for justifying the applicability and relevance of the information it seeks to rely upon, and where it does so, the NRC may be able to tailor the focus and depth of its review.

Under this approach, the NRC notes that the extent to which prior DOE or DOW information can be leveraged depends on the information an applicant provides and the associated justification as to how it meets NRC requirements. For example, the scope of a DOE or DOW authorization or associated testing may not fully represent a commercial reactor configuration or the breadth of analyses necessary for an NRC licensing basis. The final rule is intentionally structured to reflect that not all prior DOE or DOW authorizations may meet the full scope of requirements specified in § 50.43 and, in recognition of this, to nevertheless make clear that the NRC retains the flexibility to leverage information from such authorizations to the maximum extent practical.

In addition, some comments highlighted that it would be beneficial for the NRC to provide additional clarity as to the type of information an NRC application might include in a discussion of how a prior DOE or DOW authorization supports the application. In light of these comments, the NRC is clarifying that, when demonstrating compliance with applicable regulations, an NRC applicant may, for example, consider including a discussion of the following: (A) the scope of the prior authorization, including the testing, analyses, and operational experience on which it relied, as applicable; (B) the safety functions and design features addressed by such authorization; and (C) any differences from the prior authorization in design, operational, or siting factors relevant to the application that are material to the Commission's licensing review.

Finally, several commenters expressed concerns that the proposed § 50.43(e)(3) would establish a lower safety standard than that required by § 50.43(e)(1)-(2) and § 53.440(a)(1). As explained in the public comment response document, the NRC has revised the language originally proposed to be codified in § 50.43(e)(3) and has added it as a new subsection § 50.43(f) to clarify and to reaffirm that this rulemaking does not alter the safety standard established in§ 50.43(e) and to enhance consistency with the approach taken in the rule revision to § 53.440(a)(1). As such, the final rule language makes clear that § 50.43(f) is one option that a commercial license applicant may use to satisfy the requirements, or a subset of the requirements, contained in section(e). As previously noted, applicants must still meet all applicable NRC and statutory requirements. Although § 50.43(f) provides a pathway for reactor technologies to demonstrate the ability to accomplish safety functions under § 50.43(e), if an authorization does not supply all necessary information, the final rule language in § 50.43(f) reflects that applicants must address any remaining gaps to ensure full compliance with NRC requirements.

## VI. Regulatory Analysis

The NRC has not prepared a separate regulatory analysis. However, the NRC performed a qualitative economic analysis of the rule's impacts. As a result of this final rule, the NRC expects that the NRC will not need to repeat technical reviews previously performed by DOE or DOW, to the extent that those reviews cover the same scope as the related content in the NRC license application. The final rule language at § 50.43(f) offers an option to meet the requirements in § 50.43(e), and the final rule language in § 53.440(a) provides an option for how to demonstrate that the design features required by § 53.400 meet the design criteria required by §§ 53.410 and 53.420. Therefore, the NRC does not anticipate that an application under the new § 50.43(f) or the revised § 53.440(a)(1) would require more review work than under the existing requirements. Furthermore, the NRC assumes that applications under these sections will be high-quality applications.

In general, the NRC expects cost savings to both applicants in preparing an application and the NRC in reviewing that application. The NRC was not able to quantify these potential savings in a useful way at this time. These savings depend on the applicability of the DOE/DOW reviews to NRC requirements and the ability of applicants to demonstrate that applicability, the number of applications that may reference a previous DOE/DOW authorization, as well as how any potential differences ( e.g. , changes to the design or site-specific factors) from the previously authorized design also meet NRC requirements. The NRC anticipates that use of the approaches provided by § 50.43(f) or 53.440(a)(1) will become more efficient as experience is gained, i.e. , there may be an initial learning curve which may result in greater cost savings over time, to both the NRC and applicants, as a result of the more flexibility this final rule provides to applicants. Therefore, the NRC expects that this final rule will be cost beneficial to both the NRC and future applicants.

## VII. Backfitting and Issue Finality

The changes to §§ 50.43(f) and 53.440(a)(1) do not impose new or revised requirements on existing licensees or approval holders because they include an option that would apply only to future applicants. Therefore, the changes do not constitute backfitting as defined in § 50.109(a)(1) or 53.1590(a)(1) or affect the issue finality of an existing approval issued under 10 CFR part 52, “Licenses, Certifications, and Approvals for Nuclear Power Plants.”

## VIII. Cumulative Effects of Regulation

The NRC seeks to minimize any potential negative consequences resulting from the cumulative effects of regulation (CER). The CER describes the challenges that licensees, or other impacted entities such as State partners, may face while implementing new regulatory positions, programs, or requirements ( e.g. , rules, generic letters, backfits, inspections). The CER is an organizational effectiveness challenge that may result from a licensee or impacted entity implementing a number of complex regulatory actions, programs, or requirements within limited available resources.

The goals of the NRC's CER effort were met throughout the development of this final rule. The NRC engaged with external stakeholders at a public meeting and solicited public comments on the proposed rule. The NRC included in the proposed rule a request for feedback related to CER. Specifically, the NRC requested feedback on the implementation and potential unintended consequences of the proposed rule, and invited comments on the regulatory analysis. The NRC received several comment submissions in response to these CER questions, which are addressed in the public comment response document as indicated in the “Availability of Documents” section of this document.
